# Study B regionality findings

The registered 2023-2025 DGES mobility matrices show a stable and substantial same-district preference pattern, but realised placements are consistently less geographically concentrated than first choices.

Comparable district-origin coverage is high in every year and flow type, ranging from 94.7% to 95.3% of all published flows. The registered same-district first-choice shares are:

- 2023: 64.03%;
- 2024: 65.24%;
- 2025: 63.97%.

The corresponding same-district placement shares are:

- 2023: 55.55%;
- 2024: 57.26%;
- 2025: 57.87%.

Thus the placement-minus-first-choice contrast is negative in every year:

- 2023: -8.47 percentage points;
- 2024: -7.98 percentage points;
- 2025: -6.10 percentage points.

This means the realised allocation is less same-district concentrated than the expressed first-choice pattern in this window. The result is descriptive: it does not identify which mechanisms cause the difference. Grades, programme availability, capacity and competition can all constrain placement relative to first choice.

The off-diagonal structure measures point in the same direction. Conditional destination entropy is higher for placement than for first choice in all three years, while normalised mutual information is lower for placement. Realised placements are therefore more geographically dispersed and less tightly associated with origin area than first-choice flows.

The primary result is reported only for origins explicitly classified as districts. Autonomous regions remain in the full matrices and contribute to the coverage denominator; they are not relabelled as districts. The source matrices include both autonomous regions on the destination axis as required by the corrected geography contract.

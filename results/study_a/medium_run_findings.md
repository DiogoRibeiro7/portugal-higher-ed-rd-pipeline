# Study A medium-run findings

## Scope

This release extends the official broad-area first-phase series to the source-locked
years 2017, 2018, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026. The expected 2017-2026
window still lacks a locked complete broad-area table for 2019.
No value is interpolated or reconstructed for that gap.

The broad-area table reports first-choice candidates, not all programme applications.
The full registered 1997-2026 programme-level Study A therefore remains pending.

## Endpoint evidence

Registered STEM placements rise from 13,926 in 2017 to 15,181 in 2026,
an endpoint change of +9.0%.
The corresponding placement share moves from 31.01% to 30.37%,
a change of -0.64 percentage points.

Component endpoint changes are:

- group 05, natural sciences, mathematics and statistics: -6.8%;
- group 06, information and communication technologies: +60.0%;
- group 07, engineering, manufacturing and construction: +11.0%.

The source category `Engenharia e Técnicas Afins` changes by +11.7%
between the same endpoints.

## Descriptive fitted path

Across all source-locked years, the all-STEM log-linear placement slope corresponds to
+0.46% per calendar year
(descriptive R-squared 0.050). The placement-share
line changes by -0.077 percentage points per year.

The frozen sensitivity omitting 2020 and 2021 gives an all-STEM placement slope of
+0.75% per calendar year and a placement-share
gradient of -0.074 percentage points per year.

These fitted lines are compact descriptions of administrative population counts. They are
not sampling-inference estimates, causal effects, or structural-break tests.

## Interpretation boundary

The medium-run source-locked evidence does not support a simple claim that the absolute
number of first-phase STEM placements has been falling continuously since 2017. It also
does not settle the registered long-run thesis: the broad-area layer has a 2019 source gap,
does not yet extend to 1997, and does not provide programme-level applicants per vacancy or
demographic normalisation. Count trends and field shares also point to different margins and
must be reported together.

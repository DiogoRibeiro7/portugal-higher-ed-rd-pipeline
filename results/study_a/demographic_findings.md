# Study A demographic sensitivity findings

## Denominator boundary

The exact age-18 denominator is now source-locked and is the preferred Study A
demographic normalisation. This v0.3.2 result is retained as a deliberately weaker
secondary sensitivity: the INE resident population aged 15-24, disseminated by
PORDATA, divided by ten to form an average single-year cohort proxy. Competition
year t uses the population reported for t-1. This quantity is never relabelled as
the population aged 18.

## Endpoint comparison

All registered STEM placements rise by +9.0% between 2017 and 2026.
Over the corresponding previous-year population references, the 15-24 population
rises by +7.6%.
The broad-cohort-normalised STEM rate therefore changes from 126.4 to 128.1 placements per 1,000 average
single-year cohort equivalents, an endpoint change of +1.3%.

Component cohort-proxy endpoint changes are:

- group 05, natural sciences, mathematics and statistics: -13.3%;
- group 06, information and communication technologies: +48.7%;
- group 07, engineering, manufacturing and construction: +3.2%.

## Interpretation

The +9.0% raw all-STEM endpoint increase is much smaller under the broad-cohort
proxy (+1.3%), whereas the released exact age-18 normalisation is about +9.1%.
The difference shows that the broad 15-24 proxy measures young-adult population
scale rather than the registered entry-age cohort. The component divergence remains:
group 05 weakens, ICT expands strongly, and group 07 is only modestly higher under
the broad proxy.

This sensitivity does not fill the missing 2019 broad-area observation and does not
authorise causal or structural-break claims. Counts, national placement share, exact
age-18 rates and broad-cohort proxy rates remain distinct empirical margins.
